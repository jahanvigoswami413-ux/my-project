      About & model

      What this project does, how the prediction model works and how well it performs.

      The problem

      Customer churn means customers stop using a service. For a telecom company every lost customer is lost income, and winning a new customer costs far more than keeping one. RetainIQ shows who is likely to leave, why, and how much revenue is at stake, so the business can act early.

      Objectives

      • Measure churn: customers, churn rate, average bill, revenue at risk.
      • Find which customer groups leave the most.
      • Predict the churn probability of any customer.
      • Estimate the money saved if churn falls.
      • Recommend actions and export a report.

      How it works

      1. Data: each customer has gender, tenure, contract, internet service and monthly charges, plus whether they left.
      2. Analysis: filters, charts and tables compare groups.
      3. Model: logistic regression turns the five fields into a churn probability.
      4. Action: risk levels, what-if tests and recommendations.

      What drives churn?

      Glossary

      • Churn rate: share of customers who left.
      • Revenue at risk: monthly charges of the customers who left.
      • Churn probability: the model’s estimate (0–100%) that a customer will leave.
      • Risk level: Low under 30%, Medium 30–60%, High 60% or more.
      • Precision: of the customers flagged as leavers, how many really left.
      • Recall: of the customers who really left, how many the model caught.
      • ROC AUC: how well the model ranks leavers above stayers (0.5 = guessing, 1 = perfect).

      Limitations and tools

      • The sample data is synthetic. Upload a real CSV (for example the Telco churn dataset) for real results.
      • The model uses only five fields, so it is a first estimate, not a final decision.
      • It shows what is linked to churn, not proof of what causes it.
      • Built with HTML, JavaScript, Chart.js, AlaSQL and jsPDF. The model is trained in the browser on an 80/20 train/test split.
